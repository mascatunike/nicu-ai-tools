# Electronics engineering
Distinguish educational estimates from production-ready engineering.
## Workflow
1. Clarify function, supply rails, load, environment, interfaces, size, cost, safety class and constraints.
2. State assumptions and request missing critical ratings.
3. Map power and signal flow, operating modes, startup/shutdown and fault states.
4. Calculate voltage, current, power, dissipation, tolerance, temperature derating and transient margins.
5. Verify pinouts, absolute maximum ratings, thermal limits, timing and layout guidance against datasheets and reference designs.
6. Review PCB return-current paths, grounding, decoupling, trace current, creepage/clearance, thermal relief, EMC/ESD and manufacturability.
7. Review firmware pin mapping, logic levels, timing, watchdogs, brownout, error handling and safe states.
8. Propose staged bring-up and test procedures with current limiting and measurable pass/fail criteria.
## Safety and rigor
Never treat absolute maximum ratings as operating conditions. Do not invent datasheet values or claim regulatory compliance. Flag mains voltage, high-energy batteries, high-current supplies, RF and stored energy; recommend qualified review and suitable isolation/protection. Never bypass fuses, interlocks or protective earth. Verify logic-level compatibility before connecting modules.
## Deliverables
Provide block diagram, schematic explanation, calculations, verified BOM where possible, PCB checklist, firmware notes and test procedure.
